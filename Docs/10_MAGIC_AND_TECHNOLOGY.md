# 10 — MAGIC AND TECHNOLOGY

**Project:** Nomad Wars  
**Document type:** World Foundation / Worldbuilding  
**Status:** Canon — foundational design  
**Scope:** Magic, technology, their relationship with society, warfare and nomadism

---

## 1. PURPOSE OF THIS DOCUMENT

This document defines the fundamental principles governing:

- magic;
- technology;
- magical and technological development;
- their relationship with the environment;
- their use by different cultures;
- military applications;
- heroes and specialized units;
- the boundary between fantasy and technological explanations.

The purpose is not to create a complete spell list or technology tree.

The purpose is to establish the **rules of the world** from which later gameplay systems, factions, units, buildings, heroes and abilities can be designed.

---

# 2. CORE PRINCIPLE

Magic and technology are not separate game systems that exist only to provide special abilities.

They are parts of civilization.

Different cultures understand and use the forces of the world differently.

One culture may develop:

- metallurgy;
- mechanical devices;
- mobile engineering;
- ranged weapons;
- siege mechanisms.

Another may rely primarily on:

- ritual;
- spirits;
- fire;
- healing;
- environmental knowledge;
- animal relationships.

Another may combine both.

Therefore:

> **The technological and magical identity of a faction should emerge from its culture and way of life.**

The player should be able to understand something about a civilization by looking at its:

- buildings;
- weapons;
- transportation;
- armor;
- rituals;
- magical effects;
- siege equipment;
- heroes.

---

# 3. MAGIC IS REAL

Magic is a real force within the world.

It is not merely superstition or misunderstood technology.

However, magic does not automatically mean that every phenomenon can be explained or controlled.

The existence of magic does not remove the importance of:

- ecology;
- climate;
- geography;
- resources;
- logistics;
- physical limitations;
- human and animal physiology.

Magic exists **inside the world's natural order** rather than replacing it completely.

---

# 4. MAGIC IS NOT UNLIMITED

Magic has limitations.

A civilization cannot simply solve every environmental or military problem with spells.

Powerful effects should require one or more limiting factors, such as:

- trained specialists;
- rare resources;
- time;
- preparation;
- favorable environmental conditions;
- artifacts;
- rituals;
- physical or mental exhaustion;
- specific locations;
- cooperation between several practitioners.

This prevents magic from making the rest of the world irrelevant.

---

# 5. MAGIC AND THE ENVIRONMENT

The world of Nomad Wars is defined by environmental change.

Climate, seasons, resources and migration are fundamental systems.

Magic should therefore interact with the environment rather than exist completely independently from it.

Examples:

- fire magic may be especially effective in dry environments;
- water-related magic may become strategically important during drought;
- cold magic may interact with winter conditions;
- healing or plant-related magic may depend on biological resources;
- spirits may be associated with particular landscapes;
- certain magical resources may appear or disappear as a region's climate state changes (geometry stays fixed).

These relationships should be designed carefully.

Magic should **enhance the importance of the environment**, not eliminate it.
