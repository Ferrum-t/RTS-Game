# 04 — HISTORY AND ORIGIN

**Project:** Nomads Wars

**Last updated:** 2026-08-28

## 1. PURPOSE

This document defines the historical foundation of the world of **Nomads Wars**.

It explains:

- how the current world came to exist;
- how the major nomadic peoples originated;
- why different cultures developed different ways of life;
- why migration became a fundamental part of civilization;
- how the long-standing balance between peoples was maintained;
- what disrupted that balance;
- why the conflicts of the game begin.

This document defines **world history**, not detailed game missions or campaign chronology.

The exact names of peoples, historical dates, dynasties, individual rulers, and detailed events remain open for future development unless explicitly marked as canonical.

---

# 2. CORE HISTORICAL PRINCIPLE

The history of Nomads Wars is not primarily the history of expanding kingdoms.

It is the history of peoples adapting to a changing world.

For thousands of years, the inhabitants of the great continental regions developed societies whose survival depended on movement.

The environment was never completely stable.

Favorable regions appeared and disappeared.

Grasslands shifted.

Water systems changed.

Animals migrated.

Droughts and cold periods altered the distribution of life.

Some peoples followed herds.

Others followed seasonal resources.

Others established semi-permanent settlements along reliable rivers, lakes, mountains, forests, or fertile lowlands.

Over generations, these different strategies became cultures.

Therefore:

> **The cultures of the world are not arbitrary aesthetic choices. They are long-term adaptations to geography, climate, resources, and history.**

---

# 3. THE AGE OF BALANCE

Before the beginning of the game, the major peoples of the central continent lived within a relatively stable ecological and political balance.

This does not mean that the world was peaceful.

Conflicts, raids, rivalries, migrations, territorial disputes, and wars existed throughout history.

However, these conflicts were generally contained within a larger system.

Each civilization understood approximately:

- where it could survive;
- when it should move;
- which territories were dangerous;
- which territories belonged to other peoples;
- which migration routes were traditionally used;
- which resources were available in different seasons;
- which neighboring peoples could be traded with;
- which peoples were dangerous to provoke.

The world was therefore not static.

It was a constantly moving equilibrium.

---

# 4. THE GREAT MIGRATION CYCLES

The movement of the natural world became the foundation of civilization.

The major ecological zones did not simply remain fixed.

Over long periods, the distribution of:

- water;
- vegetation;
- animals;
- temperature;
- snow;
- fertile land;
- forests;
- grasslands;
- deserts;

changed across the continent.

Civilizations learned to anticipate these changes.

They developed migration routes and seasonal patterns.

A settlement was therefore not necessarily considered a permanent object.

A community could have:

- a seasonal camp;
- a winter settlement;
- a summer settlement;
- mobile livestock;
- transport animals;
- temporary defensive structures;
- mobile political centers.

The ability to move became as important as the ability to build.

---

# 5. THE FIRST PEOPLES

The exact prehistoric origin of the world's peoples is intentionally left partly undefined.

The setting contains several ancient cultural lineages that developed independently within the great continental environment.

For game-world purposes, these lineages eventually became the ancestors of the major playable civilizations.

They are not intended to be literal copies of historical Earth peoples.

Instead, they are **fantasy civilizations inspired by cultural patterns associated with the peoples who historically inhabited the Eurasian steppe and neighboring regions**.

Historical inspiration is transformed into an original fictional setting.

---

# 6. THE NORTHERN ANCIENT PEOPLES

## 6.1 Origin

One of the oldest known cultural traditions developed in the northern regions of the continent.

These peoples lived in environments dominated by:

- cold steppe;
- forest-steppe;
- tundra;
- seasonal snow;
- large migratory animals.

Their survival depended heavily on following large herds and exploiting materials available from the animals they hunted or followed.

Over generations they developed a culture based on:

- mobility;
- communal survival;
- animal knowledge;
- hunting;
- herd following;
- portable construction;
- adaptation to cold.

Their architecture made extensive use of materials obtained from enormous animals.

Bones, tusks, hide, leather, fur, sinew, and other natural materials became important structural and cultural elements.

---

## 6.2 Cultural Principle

Their civilization developed around the idea:

> **The settlement follows life.**

They do not consider movement a failure of civilization.

Movement is civilization.

A permanent structure is useful only when the environment makes it useful.

This principle later becomes one of the most distinctive philosophical differences between the major peoples.

---

## 6.3 Inspiration

This civilization contains visual and cultural inspiration from ancient steppe cultures, particularly archaeological traditions associated with the northern Eurasian steppe.

The inspiration is transformed into fantasy.

It should not be presented as a literal reconstruction of any historical archaeological culture.

---

# 7. THE SOUTHERN / EASTERN ANCIENT PEOPLES

Another major cultural lineage developed across warmer steppe, semi-desert, mountain, and oasis environments.

These societies developed different survival strategies from the northern peoples.

They became highly adapted to:

- seasonal grasslands;
- mountain valleys;
- dry regions;
- river corridors;
- long-distance movement;
- horse and animal husbandry;
- warfare and hunting.

Their material culture became more elaborate.

They developed distinctive artistic traditions based on:

- animal motifs;
- symbolic creatures;
- metalworking;
- decorated weapons;
- jewelry;
- ceremonial objects;
- highly recognizable clothing and equipment.

Their art emphasized animals and transformation rather than simple geometric decoration.

---

# 8. THE AGE OF THE GREAT HORSE CULTURES

The domestication and widespread use of horses fundamentally changed civilization.

The horse became more than an animal.

It became:

- transportation;
- economic wealth;
- military power;
- status;
- communication;
- migration capability;
- trade infrastructure.

This created a civilization in which distance became less restrictive.

A community with horses could move farther.

A military force with horses could respond faster.

A trading group with horses could connect distant regions.

A people without sufficient horses became increasingly vulnerable.

This eventually establishes an important principle of Nomads Wars:

> **Animals are part of the economy, but some animals are also infrastructure.**

---

# 9. THE HORSE AS A RESOURCE

Horses occupy a special position in the world.

Unlike ordinary raw materials, horses can become an active part of society.

A horse may represent:

- economic value;
- transportation capacity;
- military potential;
- logistical capacity;
- social status;
- migration capability.

Therefore, losing a herd can be more serious than simply losing a quantity of food.

A civilization may still possess soldiers but lose the ability to move them effectively.

This concept becomes an important gameplay foundation.

---

# 10. THE CENTRAL STEPPE PEOPLES

In the central regions of the continent, another major cultural tradition developed.

These peoples adapted to broad continental grasslands and harsher seasonal variations.

Their societies became known for:

- practical construction;
- strong communal organization;
- mobile settlements;
- animal husbandry;
- warfare;
- endurance;
- resourcefulness.

Compared with some wealthier southern cultures, their material culture was often more utilitarian.

Their architecture favored:

- strong frames;
- simple forms;
- durable materials;
- modular construction;
- practical defensive structures.

Their philosophy can be summarized as:

> **Survive first. Decorate later.**

This civilization forms one of the historical foundations for the later central nomadic powers.

---

# 11. THE LATER GREAT NOMADIC TRADITIONS

Over centuries, different nomadic traditions interacted.

They traded.

They fought.

They migrated through each other's territories.

They absorbed technologies and customs.

They formed alliances.

They separated.

New political identities emerged from older cultural foundations.

As a result, the world does not contain four completely isolated civilizations.

The cultures share ancient roots and influence one another.

Differences developed primarily through:

- geography;
- historical experience;
- economic specialization;
- political organization;
- religious and philosophical traditions;
- military development;
- access to resources.

---

# 12. THE FOUR MAJOR PLAYABLE CULTURAL LINEAGES

The initial game contains four major cultural lineages.

Their final names are **not yet canonical**.

Temporary development labels are used below.

---

## 12.1 ANCIENT NORTHERN LINEAGE

**Development label:** Andronovo-inspired  
**Geographical tendency:** Northern steppe / forest-steppe / tundra  
**Primary adaptation:** Following large animals and seasonal resources

### Cultural characteristics

- extremely strong relationship with animals;
- cold-weather survival;
- large communal structures;
- extensive use of bone, tusk, fur, leather, and wood;
- highly mobile society;
- strong hunting traditions;
- practical spirituality connected to nature and animals.

### Visual identity

- mammoth-like creatures;
- tusks and bone;
- fur;
- heavy fabrics;
- pale natural materials;
- large portable structures;
- robust silhouettes.

The giant animals accompanying them are fictional species.

They should not simply be literal mammoths unless later worldbuilding explicitly establishes them as such.

---

## 12.2 SOUTHERN / EASTERN ARTISTIC LINEAGE

**Development label:** Saka-inspired  
**Geographical tendency:** Steppe / mountains / semi-desert / southern regions  
**Primary adaptation:** Mobility combined with wealth, trade, warfare, and animal husbandry

### Cultural characteristics

- elaborate metalworking;
- animal-style artistic tradition;
- ceremonial objects;
- skilled mounted warfare;
- strong warrior traditions;
- high-status heroes;
- symbolic relationship with fire, animals, sun, and sky.

### Visual identity

Primary visual palette:

- gold;
- red;
- black;
- white.

Forms tend toward:

- elongated silhouettes;
- decorated structures;
- animal motifs;
- metal ornament;
- ceremonial architecture.

Possible hero archetypes include:

- golden warrior;
- fire-associated mage;
- female warrior inspired by the archetype of Tomyris;
- mounted hunters.

These are fictional characters.

Historical figures may serve as conceptual inspiration but should not be copied directly.

---

## 12.3 CENTRAL WARLIKE LINEAGE

**Development label:** Hunnic-inspired  
**Geographical tendency:** Central continental steppe  
**Primary adaptation:** Mobility, endurance, warfare, and practical construction

### Cultural characteristics

- highly practical settlements;
- strong military traditions;
- durable equipment;
- emphasis on endurance;
- strong mounted warfare;
- communal organization;
- less emphasis on luxury.

### Visual identity

Architecture may use:

- massive supports;
- compact structures;
- rough wood;
- leather;
- heavy textiles;
- dark and earthy materials.

The visual language may contain distant references to the robust silhouettes of fantasy orcs/dwarves without directly copying either.

---

## 12.4 TURAN / GREAT STEPPE LINEAGE

**Development label:** Turan-inspired / broader Turkic-nomadic synthesis  
**Status:** Initial MVP playable civilization

**Geographical tendency:** Central and eastern steppe, plains, semi-desert, mountain corridors

### Cultural characteristics

This civilization represents the most recognizable expression of the game's broader nomadic philosophy.

Its society is built around:

- seasonal movement;
- horses;
- mobile settlements;
- clan and tribal organization;
- mounted warfare;
- trade;
- scouting;
- adaptation to changing environmental conditions.

### Architecture

Characteristic elements include:

- yurts;
- large khan's tent / mobile headquarters;
- mobile watchtowers;
- wagon-based structures;
- portable workshops;
- modular settlement components.

The architecture should not be a literal historical reconstruction.

It is a fantasy reinterpretation of nomadic architecture.

### Gameplay philosophy

The Turan civilization is intended to be the clearest expression of:

> **Mobility as strength.**

Its buildings can be partially mobile.

Its economy is strongly connected to animals.

Its military can exploit movement.

Its settlements can relocate when environmental conditions change.

---

# 13. WHY THE CULTURES DO NOT ALL LOOK THE SAME

The four civilizations occupy different ecological and historical niches.

Therefore, their architecture, equipment, military doctrine, and economy should not be differentiated only through color.

Their differences should emerge from their environments and histories.

For example:

| Factor | Cultural consequence |
|---|---|
| Cold climate | Fur, insulation, compact structures |
| Large migratory animals | Animal-following culture |
| Open steppe | Mounted warfare |
| Mountains | Specialized routes and settlements |
| Desert | Water management and efficient migration |
| Rich metal resources | Elaborate weapons and ornament |
| Long-distance trade | Advanced transport |
| Frequent warfare | Defensive mobile structures |
| Seasonal resources | Migration-based economy |

This principle should guide future faction design.

---

# 14. THE LONG BALANCE

For many generations, the four major cultural regions remained within a relatively stable balance.

The ecological world moved.

The peoples moved with it.

When one region became unfavorable, populations migrated toward another.

Neighboring peoples were forced to adapt.

Sometimes migration caused conflict.

Sometimes trade solved the problem.

Sometimes temporary alliances formed.

The system was imperfect but self-correcting.

No single civilization could permanently dominate the entire continent.

The environment itself prevented complete stability.

---

# 15. SETTLED CIVILIZATIONS

Not every society in the world is nomadic.

Permanent and semi-permanent civilizations exist around:

- major rivers;
- fertile valleys;
- lakes;
- coastal regions;
- mountain passes;
- mineral deposits;
- agricultural zones.

These civilizations developed:

- farms;
- permanent towns;
- fortified settlements;
- workshops;
- markets;
- taverns;
- temples;
- trading posts.

They are important parts of the world.

They may function in gameplay as:

- neutral settlements;
- trading locations;
- mercenary camps;
- resource centers;
- quest locations;
- diplomatic locations;
- sources of specialized technology.

They are not necessarily weaker than nomadic civilizations.

They simply follow a different economic model.

---

# 16. THE OCEAN AND THE UNKNOWN WORLD

A large portion of the planet is covered by ocean.

Most of it remains poorly explored.

The major continental civilizations know only fragments of the world beyond their familiar waters.

Legends speak of:

- distant continents;
- unknown civilizations;
- island empires;
- strange creatures;
- ancient ruins;
- unexplored lands;
- civilizations separated by enormous distances.

These legends are intentionally left unresolved.

They provide space for future world expansion.

The existence of other continents should not automatically imply that all legends are true.

Some may be misunderstood.

Some may be distorted history.

Some may be completely false.

---

# 17. THE ISLAND AND MARITIME POWERS

Several island and coastal civilizations developed strong maritime traditions.

Their societies are less dependent on continental migration.

They rely more heavily on:

- fishing;
- agriculture;
- maritime trade;
- shipbuilding;
- coastal resources;
- naval warfare.

Their political and economic systems may therefore differ substantially from the nomadic civilizations.

They can become important future factions.

They are not part of the initial MVP.

---

# 18. THE SOUTHERN EXTREME CONTINENT

Far to the south lies a relatively small but extremely hostile continent.

Its geography is unusual because it extends across dramatically different climatic zones.

Some regions approach polar conditions.

Other regions contain intense volcanic activity.

The continent contains:

- glaciers;
- permafrost;
- volcanic fields;
- lava flows;
- geothermal regions;
- extreme temperature gradients.

In the coldest regions, temperatures can become extraordinarily low.

In volcanic regions, temperatures can become lethal.

Permanent civilization is extremely difficult.

The continent is therefore poorly populated.

Its true significance is unknown.

It may eventually become connected to deeper world mythology.

---

# 19. THE BEGINNING OF THE CRISIS

The central conflict of Nomads Wars begins when the long-standing balance starts to fail.

The environmental cycles themselves are not necessarily new.

The peoples have survived environmental change for generations.

The problem is that something changes the system faster or more radically than the civilizations can adapt.

Possible causes remain intentionally unresolved.

Candidate explanations include:

- an ancient power awakening;
- a supernatural disturbance;
- ecological disruption;
- the arrival of an external civilization;
- migration from an unknown continent;
- changes in climate or planetary systems;
- ancient technology returning;
- several causes occurring simultaneously.

No single explanation is currently canonical.

The important canonical fact is:

> **Something disrupts the balance that allowed the civilizations to coexist for generations.**

---

# 20. THE ARRIVAL OF OUTSIDERS

One possible major historical development is the arrival of peoples from beyond the known world.

These outsiders may originate across the ocean or from previously unknown territories.

Their arrival creates a chain reaction.

They occupy territory.

Local populations are displaced.

Displaced populations move into neighboring territories.

Neighboring peoples respond by moving again.

Eventually:

```text
OUTSIDE PRESSURE
       ↓
DISPLACEMENT
       ↓
MIGRATION
       ↓
BORDER VIOLATION
       ↓
RESOURCE COMPETITION
       ↓
CONFLICT
       ↓
WAR
````

This is one of the possible foundations of the game's initial geopolitical crisis.

The outsiders themselves are not yet fully defined.

---

# 21. THE CENTRAL TRAGEDY

The conflict of Nomads Wars should not be based on the idea that one civilization is inherently evil.

The fundamental problem is scarcity and displacement.

A people may invade another territory not because they want conquest, but because:

> **their own territory can no longer support them.**

The people they displace may have exactly the same problem.

This creates morally complicated conflicts.

One civilization's survival may become another civilization's catastrophe.

Therefore:

> **The enemy is not always the one who started the migration.**

This principle should influence the future campaign narrative.

---

# 22. WAR AS A CONSEQUENCE OF MIGRATION

War is therefore not completely separate from the environment.

The causal chain is:

```text
PLANET
   ↓
CLIMATE
   ↓
ENVIRONMENT
   ↓
RESOURCE DISTRIBUTION
   ↓
MIGRATION
   ↓
TERRITORIAL PRESSURE
   ↓
RESOURCE COMPETITION
   ↓
POLITICAL CONFLICT
   ↓
WAR
```

This chain is one of the fundamental narrative principles of Nomads Wars.

---

# 23. THE NEW AGE

The beginning of the game represents the transition from the old balance into a new historical era.

The old rules no longer work reliably.

Traditional migration routes become unsafe.

Resources appear in unexpected places.

Old borders become meaningless.

Peoples encounter enemies they did not previously know.

Animals migrate differently.

Settlements must move farther.

Trade routes collapse or change.

Political alliances become unstable.

The civilizations are forced to reconsider the assumptions that supported them for generations.

---

# 24. THE PLAYER'S HISTORICAL POSITION

The player does not begin as the creator of civilization.

The civilization already exists.

The player inherits:

* an established culture;
* existing traditions;
* known migration practices;
* military knowledge;
* technologies;
* economic systems;
* relationships with neighboring peoples.

The player's role is to lead one society through an unprecedented period of instability.

This distinction is important.

The game is not:

> "Build civilization from nothing."

It is:

> **"Lead an existing mobile society through a world whose old rules are beginning to fail."**

---

# 25. HISTORY AND GAMEPLAY

The history should directly support gameplay mechanics.

| Historical principle   | Gameplay expression                             |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Environmental cycles   | Moving ecological zones                         |
| Nomadic lifestyle      | Mobile settlements                              |
| Horse culture          | Horses as resources and military infrastructure |
| Resource scarcity      | Migration and competition                       |
| Raiding traditions     | Loot / raid mechanics                           |
| Mobile warfare         | Mounted and mobile armies                       |
| Seasonal adaptation    | Changing unit/economy conditions                |
| Different environments | Faction specialization                          |
| Displacement           | Dynamic territorial conflicts                   |
| Settled civilizations  | Neutral settlements and trading locations       |
| Ancient traditions     | Heroes, artifacts, technologies                 |
| Unstable world         | Long-term campaign progression                  |

---

# 26. RAIDING AND LOOT

Raiding is a historical and gameplay expression of the nomadic economy.

A successful raid can provide:

* resources;
* animals;
* equipment;
* information;
* temporary economic advantage.

However, raiding should not automatically equal total destruction.

The purpose of a raid may be:

* obtaining supplies;
* stealing livestock;
* weakening an enemy;
* disrupting migration;
* capturing transport;
* forcing an enemy to relocate.

This creates a strategic alternative to total conquest.

---

# 27. CAPTURE OF ANIMALS

Animals can become strategic objectives.

For example, if an enemy settlement contains valuable horses, destroying the settlement may not be the optimal action.

A player may instead attempt to:

1. attack the settlement;
2. damage or destroy its stable;
3. gain access to the animals;
4. capture or steal them;
5. use them to strengthen their own economy or military.

This establishes the conceptual connection:

```text
HORSES
  ↕
ECONOMY
  ↕
MOBILITY
  ↕
MILITARY
  ↕
RAIDING
```

This mechanic is especially appropriate for the nomadic civilizations.

---

# 28. MOBILE SETTLEMENTS IN HISTORICAL CONTEXT

Mobile buildings are not simply a gameplay gimmick.

They represent a civilization in which infrastructure itself has adapted to movement.

Important structures may therefore exist in two fundamental conditions:

```text
DEPLOYED
   ↕
MOBILE
```

A deployed structure provides greater functionality and defensive capability.

A mobile structure sacrifices efficiency in exchange for movement.

The most important example is the mobile political center / khan's headquarters.

The settlement can be raised and moved when necessary.

This reflects the historical principle:

> **A nomadic society does not abandon civilization when it moves. It takes civilization with it.**

---

# 29. THE PHILOSOPHY OF MOVEMENT

The civilizations of Nomads Wars do not necessarily perceive movement as instability.

For them:

* movement can be safety;
* movement can be prosperity;
* movement can be adaptation;
* movement can be military strategy;
* movement can be tradition.

A stationary enemy may possess stronger fortifications.

A mobile enemy may possess greater freedom.

Neither philosophy is inherently superior.

The strategic question is:

> **When should a civilization remain, and when should it move?**

This question should remain central to the game.

# 31. CANONICAL RULES

The following principles are currently considered canonical:

1. The world is a fantasy world inspired in part by cultures historically associated with Central Asia and the Eurasian steppe.

2. The major civilizations are fictional and are not direct representations of historical ethnic groups.

3. The civilizations developed different cultures partly because of different environmental conditions.

4. Nomadism is an adaptation to the world, not merely an aesthetic choice.

5. Environmental change is a fundamental force in history.

6. Migration has always existed.

7. The major peoples historically maintained a relatively stable balance despite conflicts.

8. The current era begins when that balance is disrupted.

9. Resource competition and displacement are major causes of the coming wars.

10. Not every enemy is inherently evil.

11. Horses and other animals can function simultaneously as resources, economic assets, transport, and military infrastructure.

12. Mobile settlements are a cultural and economic consequence of nomadic civilization.

13. Settled civilizations also exist and are important to the world.

14. The world beyond the main continent is intentionally only partially known.

15. Additional continents and civilizations remain available for future development.

16. The exact cause of the global disruption is currently unresolved.

17. The final names of the four major playable civilizations are not yet fixed.

---

# 32. OPEN QUESTIONS

The following questions remain intentionally unresolved:

### Origins

* Did all intelligent peoples share a common ancestor?
* Were the major civilizations originally related?
* Did supernatural forces influence their development?
* Are the oldest legends historically accurate?

### The Balance

* Why did the ecological cycles remain relatively stable for so long?
* Was the balance natural?
* Did an ancient civilization influence it?
* Was the balance already beginning to fail before the current crisis?

### The Disruption

* What exactly changed?
* Is the change natural or artificial?
* Is there one cause or several?
* Is the awakening of an ancient power connected to the environmental instability?

### The Outsiders

* Where did they come from?
* Why did they leave their homeland?
* Are they invaders, refugees, colonists, or something else?
* Did they know what awaited them?
* Are they responsible for the disruption?

### The Unknown World

* What lies beyond the ocean?
* Are the legendary continents real?
* Why is communication between distant civilizations so difficult?
* What civilizations have evolved in isolation?

These questions should remain open until the worldbuilding process establishes answers.

---

# 33. DESIGN PRINCIPLE

The history of Nomads Wars should always preserve the following relationship:

> **The world creates pressure.
> Pressure creates movement.
> Movement creates contact.
> Contact creates conflict.
> Conflict creates history.**

The player then participates in that history.

The world should never feel like a static stage on which wars happen.

The world itself is one of the forces causing the wars.
