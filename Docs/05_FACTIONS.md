# 05 — FACTIONS

## Status

**Document type:** World Foundation / Faction Design  
**Status:** FOUNDATION — living document  
**Scope:** Four primary playable cultures of Nomads Wars  
**Current MVP faction:** Turan  
**Canon level:** Core concepts are fixed; names, exact history, unit roster, architecture and mythology remain expandable.

---

# 1. PURPOSE

The world of Nomads Wars is inhabited by several major cultures that evolved under different environmental conditions and developed different answers to the same fundamental problem:

> **How does a society survive in a world that never remains stable?**

The factions are not intended to be simple visual variations of the same nomadic civilization.

Their differences should emerge from:

- environment;
- migration patterns;
- economy;
- relationship with animals;
- architecture;
- technology;
- military doctrine;
- social organization;
- spiritual beliefs;
- relationship with territory;
- understanding of survival.

The visual language of the factions draws inspiration from historical cultures of the Eurasian steppe, including cultures associated with the territory of modern Kazakhstan.

These inspirations are **not intended as direct historical reconstructions**.

The game uses transformed cultural motifs as a fantasy foundation.

The objective is not:

> "Put historical Kazakh objects into a fantasy game."

The objective is:

> **Transform the logic, aesthetics and worldview of steppe civilizations into fantasy cultures that feel internally coherent.**

---

# 2. THE FOUR PRIMARY CULTURES

The initial world contains four major playable nomadic cultures.

Their working inspirations are:

| Faction | Historical inspiration | Environmental tendency | General fantasy direction | MVP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Faction I | Andronovo-related steppe cultures | Northern / cold steppe | Ancient beast-following nomads | No |
| Faction II | Saka / Scythian cultural sphere | Steppe / forest-steppe | Beast-style aristocratic civilization | No |
| Faction III | Hunnic / early nomadic traditions | Central steppe | Harsh militarized nomads | No |
| Faction IV | Turkic / broader Eurasian nomadic traditions | Central and southern steppe | Classic mobile nomadic civilization | **Yes** |

The historical inspirations are deliberately combined into a single fantasy timeline.

The factions do **not** exist in the same chronological sequence as their real-world inspirations.

They coexist within the fictional world.

---

# 3. CORE DESIGN PRINCIPLE

The factions should not be differentiated primarily through:

- color;
- architecture shape;
- unit statistics;
- weapon models;
- fantasy races.

They should first be differentiated through their **relationship with the world**.

A useful design test is:

> If the faction's buildings, colors and names were removed, could its gameplay still reveal something about its culture?

If yes, the faction has a strong identity.

If no, the faction is primarily cosmetic.

---

# 4. THE SHARED NOMADIC FOUNDATION

All four major cultures share several fundamental characteristics.

They have developed societies capable of:

- moving settlements;
- transporting resources;
- maintaining livestock;
- organizing mobile armies;
- following changing environmental conditions;
- exploiting temporary resource zones;
- adapting to seasonal changes;
- using mobile infrastructure;
- conducting raids;
- trading;
- defending migration routes.

However, they do not all practice nomadism in the same way.

There is no single "correct" form of nomadic society.

Some cultures:

- follow herds;
- follow seasonal vegetation;
- follow water;
- migrate according to climate;
- maintain semi-permanent settlements;
- move only their political center;
- move military infrastructure;
- maintain permanent structures alongside mobile settlements.

This distinction is important.

Nomad Wars is not intended to represent:

> "Four versions of the same nomad."

It represents:

> **Four different civilizations that developed within a world where mobility is necessary.**

# 5. FACTION I — ANDRONOVO-INSPIRED CULTURE

## Working designation

**Andronovo**

This is a temporary design designation.

The final fantasy name must be developed later.

---

## 5.1. Core concept

The oldest-feeling of the four major cultures.

Their society developed in the northern regions of the continent, where cold steppe, forest-steppe and tundra create difficult conditions.

They are associated with enormous migratory beasts resembling mammoths.

These creatures are not simply mounts.

They are part of the ecological and economic foundation of the civilization.

The people follow the migration of these gigantic animals.

The relationship is therefore:

```text
Climate
   ↓
Vegetation
   ↓
Great Herds
   ↓
Migration
   ↓
Nomadic settlements
   ↓
Society
```

The civilization has developed around the idea that the world itself must be followed rather than conquered.

---

## 5.2. Relationship with giant beasts

The giant elephant/mammoth-like creatures are a fundamental part of the culture.

They can provide:

* transportation;
* materials;
* food;
* protection;
* social status;
* construction materials;
* military potential.

Their tusks, bones, hide and hair can become important materials.

Possible construction materials include:

* bone;
* ivory-like material;
* massive hides;
* wool/fur;
* wood;
* stone.

These materials should create a recognizable architecture without directly copying real historical structures.

---

## 5.3. Philosophy

Their worldview can be summarized as:

> **The world moves, therefore we move.**

They do not necessarily regard permanence as the highest form of civilization.

A settlement is valuable because it supports the people, not because it occupies a particular piece of land forever.

The migration of the great beasts may be understood almost as a natural calendar.

The movement of the herd determines:

* when to leave;
* where to camp;
* where resources will appear;
* where enemies may be encountered.

---

## 5.4. Gameplay identity

Potential characteristics:

* strong mobility;
* strong interaction with animals;
* high survival capability in cold environments;
* mobile infrastructure;
* strong transport capacity;
* powerful heavy units;
* lower technological sophistication;
* dependence on animal resources.

Their economy should feel strongly connected to the ecosystem.

---

## 5.5. Visual language

General visual direction:

* massive;
* organic;
* ancient;
* wool;
* bone;
* hide;
* wood;
* stone;
* large silhouettes;
* rounded structures;
* heavy materials.

The faction should feel like it has existed for an extremely long time.

# 8. FACTION IV — TURAN-INSPIRED CULTURE

## Working designation

**Turan**

This is currently the primary MVP faction.

The final name remains subject to change.

The faction represents a broader fantasy interpretation of later Turkic and Eurasian nomadic traditions.

---

# 9. TURAN — CORE IDENTITY

Turan represents the most recognizable expression of the Nomads Wars concept:

> **A highly mobile society whose settlement, army and economy are designed to move together.**

This faction should become the first complete demonstration of the game's central philosophy.

---

## 9.1. Central principle

The Turan civilization does not think of a settlement as a fixed city.

Instead:

> **The settlement is a temporary concentration of society.**

A village, camp or capital can be:

* deployed;
* packed;
* transported;
* redeployed.

This creates the central gameplay relationship:

```text
Environment changes
        ↓
Resources change
        ↓
Migration becomes necessary
        ↓
Settlement moves
        ↓
Economy changes
        ↓
Military position changes
        ↓
Conflict emerges
```

---

# 10. TURAN AND MOBILE SETTLEMENTS

The Town Center is not simply a building.

It represents the political and social center of the mobile society.

Possible form:

**Khan's headquarters / mobile royal tent / large ceremonial yurt mounted on a transport platform.**

The exact visual design remains open.

---

## 10.1. Settlement states

A major settlement may have two fundamental states:

### DEPLOYED

The settlement is fully established.

Advantages may include:

* higher armor;
* full production;
* full resource processing;
* full defensive capability;
* access to complete technologies.

### MOBILE

The settlement is packed for migration.

Possible effects:

* lower armor;
* reduced or disabled production;
* reduced defensive capability;
* slower movement;
* transport animation;
* different collision footprint.

The exact numerical modifiers are gameplay parameters and are not fixed by this document.

---

## 10.2. Raising the settlement

The Town Center can provide two different commands.

### Raise Settlement

Moves only the Town Center.

Purpose:

* reposition the political center;
* establish a new base;
* react to environmental change;
* escape danger.

### Raise Entire Settlement

Orders mobile structures associated with the settlement to enter migration mode.

Purpose:

* complete relocation;
* strategic migration;
* emergency evacuation.

The distinction is important because not every building must be mobile.

**Implementation note (v1.0 code, 2026-08-28):** the game currently implements only **Raise Entire Settlement** behaviour — empty unit selection + RMB ground moves **all** team-0 MOBILE buildings (Formation-offsets). **Raise Settlement (TC only)** is not selectable yet; distinguish as separate commands after Environment Zones. See `nomad_wars_v1_scope_and_architecture.md`.

A mobile tower can:

* provide stronger defense when deployed;

# 15. HORSES AS A STRATEGIC RESOURCE

Horses are not simply another resource counter.

They represent:

* transportation;
* mobility;
* military potential;
* logistics;
* social wealth;
* future cavalry capacity.

At the beginning of the game, the player may need to capture wild horses.

This makes the early game fundamentally different from a standard RTS economy.

Instead of:

> Gather resources → build army.

The beginning can become:

> Establish survival → find animals → capture horses → secure mobility → establish military capacity.

---

# 16. HORSES AND MILITARY CAPACITY

The number of available horses can influence the amount and/or type of mobile military forces the player can maintain.

This must be implemented carefully.

Horses should not simply become an arbitrary second population counter.

Their role should be justified by the world's economy.

Potential relationship:

```text
Wild horses
     ↓
Capture
     ↓
Domestication
     ↓
Herd
     ↓
Transport / logistics
     ↓
Mounted warfare
```

The exact population and production formulas are gameplay-system decisions.

# 17. RAIDING AND LOOT

Raiding is one of the major strategic expressions of nomadic warfare.

All major factions may eventually have access to some form of:

> **Loot / Raid**

mechanic.

This is inspired conceptually by the Plunder ability in Warcraft III, but should become a broader Nomads Wars system.

---

## 17.1. Raiding buildings

Attacking enemy infrastructure may generate strategic rewards.

Possible targets:

* resource buildings;
* warehouses;
* animal structures;
* military infrastructure;
* settlements.

The purpose is not simply:

> "Deal more damage to buildings."

Instead:

> **A successful raid can temporarily transfer part of the opponent's economic advantage.**

---

# 18. ANIMAL RAIDING

Animal infrastructure can become a special strategic target.

For example:

If an enemy stable is destroyed or captured, animals stored there may become available to the attacker.

Potential mechanic:

1. Destroy or capture stable.
2. Animal stock becomes vulnerable.
3. Specialized unit or worker interacts with it.
4. Animals are captured.
5. Captured animals are transferred to the player's economy.

This creates an unusual strategic choice:

> Do I destroy the enemy's animal infrastructure, or do I capture its economic value?

The mechanic should be designed later in a dedicated economy/raiding document.

# 30. FACTIONS AND THE MOVING WORLD

The most important shared system is the changing environment.

The world does not remain static.

Environmental zones can move across the map.

Possible states include:

* favorable;
* transitional;
* drought;
* cold.

The exact final terminology is defined in the world/environment documents.

The factions respond differently to these changes.

For example:

```text
Environment changes
        ↓
Resource distribution changes
        ↓
Faction response
        ↓
Migration
        ↓
Contact
        ↓
Competition
        ↓
Conflict
```

This means factions are not simply fighting over static territory.

They are competing for access to a changing world.

---

# 31. TERRITORY

Territory should not be understood exclusively as:

> "The land inside my borders."

For nomadic societies, territory is better understood as a network of:

* migration routes;
* water sources;
* grazing areas;
* resource zones;
* strategic passes;
* seasonal camps;
* trade routes;
* sacred places;
* settlements.

Therefore:

> **Control of movement can be as important as control of land.**

This principle should influence future mechanics.

# 32. FACTIONS AND CONFLICT

The four cultures historically developed systems that allowed them to coexist.

Their migration patterns occupied different environmental and geographic niches.

For centuries, a dynamic balance existed.

The balance is now breaking.

The exact external cause is still being developed.

Potential causes include:

* an ancient evil awakening;
* arrival of foreign peoples from across the ocean;
* ecological disruption;
* supernatural change;
* technological intrusion;
* changing migration patterns;
* combination of several events.

No single explanation should be treated as final until the main world history document establishes it.

---

# 33. THE CENTRAL CONFLICT

The fundamental conflict is not simply:

> Good faction vs evil faction.

Instead:

> **A previously stable system of migration and coexistence is being disrupted.**

One people moves because its environment becomes hostile.

That movement enters another people's territory.

The second people is forced to move.

Its migration collides with a third society.

The resulting conflict produces:

```text
Environmental pressure
        ↓
Migration
        ↓
Territorial overlap
        ↓
Resource competition
        ↓
Raiding
        ↓
Military escalation
        ↓
War
```

The players therefore participate in a conflict whose original cause is larger than any single faction.

---

# 34. THE PHILOSOPHICAL ROLE OF FACTIONS

Each faction should eventually represent a different answer to the same philosophical question:

> **How should intelligent life respond to a world that cannot be controlled?**

Possible answers:

### Andronovo

> Follow the world.

### Saka

> Understand and master the forces of the world.

### Hunnic

> Endure the world.

### Turan

> Adapt to the world.

These formulations are provisional philosophical anchors, not final faction slogans.

They should guide future development.

---

# 35. CULTURAL INSPIRATION RULE

Historical cultures are sources of inspiration, not templates.

Do not directly reproduce:

* historical clothing;
* exact weapons;
* historical architecture;
* real religious systems;
* historical political structures;
* famous individuals.

Instead, extract deeper principles.

For example:

```text
Historical motif
      ↓
Underlying principle
      ↓
Fantasy transformation
      ↓
Gameplay expression
```

Example:

```text
Nomadic mobility
      ↓
Settlements must follow resources
      ↓
Mobile Town Center
      ↓
Migration becomes gameplay
```

Another:

```text
Horse culture
      ↓
Mobility + wealth + warfare
      ↓
Horses become strategic resource
      ↓
Cavalry + logistics + raiding
```

Another:

```text
Animal Style
      ↓
Animals as symbols of power
      ↓
Fantasy animal-based heraldry
      ↓
Hero / clan / magic identities
```

---

# 36. WHAT MAKES NOMADS WARS DIFFERENT

The factions should ultimately reinforce the central statement of the game:

> **The map changes the players.**

The player is not merely building an empire on a fixed landscape.

The player is managing a society inside a moving ecological system.

The faction determines:

> **how that society responds to change.**

* basic military (Worker, Soldier, Cavalry, Siege);
* raiding / plunder foundation;
* basic mobile defensive structure (Watchtower).

**Explicitly NOT v1.0 MVP (backlog — do not implement because this lore doc mentions them):**

* hero system (XP, abilities, auras, inventory, archetypes in §23–26);
* playable non-Turan factions;
* full Capture/Steal of animals beyond current horse resource node;
* Raise Settlement (TC only) as a separate command from Raise Entire Settlement — see §10.2 note.

The other factions do not need implementation yet.

Their worldbuilding can continue independently.

---

# 38. MVP DESIGN PRINCIPLE

The first faction should not attempt to demonstrate every possible feature.

The purpose of Turan is to answer one question:

> **Is Nomads Wars fun when the player must live in a changing world instead of simply expanding across a static map?**

If the answer is yes, additional factions can be built around different solutions to the same world.

# 39. FUTURE FACTIONS

The four primary cultures are not necessarily the complete population of the world.

Future civilizations may include:

* northern peoples;
* polar civilizations;
* desert societies;
* island kingdoms;
* maritime states;
* deep-sea civilizations;
* supernatural races;
* undead;
* demons;
* otherworldly peoples;
* civilizations from distant continents.

These should be introduced only when the core world and four primary cultures are sufficiently established.

---

# 40. DESIGN RULE — NO FACTION WITHOUT A REASON

Every major faction mechanic should answer at least one of these questions:

1. Why does this culture need it?
2. Why did this culture develop it?
3. What environmental pressure produced it?
4. What social structure supports it?
5. What resource makes it possible?
6. How does it affect migration?
7. How does it affect warfare?

If a mechanic cannot answer these questions, it should be reconsidered.

# 43. FINAL PRINCIPLE

The factions of Nomads Wars are not four skins placed on the same RTS system.

They are four different answers to the same environmental reality.

The world changes.

Resources move.

Animals migrate.

Climate shifts.

Settlements cannot always remain where they were built.

Societies therefore move.

Movement creates encounters.

Encounters create competition.

Competition creates conflict.

And conflict creates the game.

The ultimate faction design goal is:

> **When the player chooses a faction, they should not merely choose an army. They should choose a different way of surviving the world.**
